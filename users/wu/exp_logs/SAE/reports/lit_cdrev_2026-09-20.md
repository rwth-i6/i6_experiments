# Context-dependent reverse emission p_phi(units | k, h): what the literature says
Date 2026-09-20. All claims verified from full text (PDF -> text, greps quoted below), never abstracts.

## Q1. Does conditioning the acoustic/generative side on the previous unit change WHAT is discovered?

**Yeh, Tang, Lu, Lee, Yu (ICLR 2019), "Unsupervised speech recognition via segmental empirical output distribution matching", arXiv:1812.09323.** The only published *direct* mono-vs-tri contrast inside an unsupervised phone-recognition pipeline. Table 2 (TIMIT, 5-gram phone LM, N=5; verified against ar5iv HTML):

| stage | matching-LM PER | non-matching-LM PER |
|---|---|---|
| 2nd iteration (unsup. objective) | 42.6 | 49.1 |
| + HMM (mono) | 41.5 | 44.7 |
| + HMM (tri) | 39.4 | **44.9** |
| + HMM (tri + SAT) | 36.5 | 41.6 |

**Negative result:** in the *non-matching* LM condition (text corpus disjoint from the audio corpus — our condition), monophone -> triphone is 44.7 -> 44.9, i.e. context-dependent emissions bought nothing. It bought 2.1 pts only when the LM was fit on transcripts of an overlapping corpus. What did buy 3.3 pts there was SAT (speaker adaptation = removing a *nuisance* factor), not phonetic context. Also: the triphone models are applied *after* the unsupervised objective has already produced a ~43% PER (phone-like) code; they are a self-training back-end, never the mechanism that makes the code phone-like. Their own ablations credit (a) the frame-smoothness term (lambda=0 "degrades significantly", Fig 3a) and (b) boundary refinement (49.1 -> 42.6 in one iteration).

**Lee & Glass (ACL 2012), "A Nonparametric Bayesian Approach to Acoustic Model Discovery", https://aclanthology.org/P12-1005.pdf.** Emissions are context-INDEPENDENT 3-state HMMs. Verbatim: "cluster 19 is mapped to /ae/ followed by stop conso-nants, while cluster 20 corresponds to /ae/ followed by nasal consonants. This context-dependent relationship is also observed in other English phones". So a context-independent generative model does not *fail* on coarticulation — it absorbs it by *splitting the inventory allophonically*. This is the one genuine pro-argument for our change: with a fixed |K|=phone-inventory, a context-independent p_phi has to spend units on context, and factoring context out frees them. But note the failure it predicts is an allophonically-split code, NOT a content-free one. Our observed failure (PER 0.90, token sequence not phone-like) is not the failure this mechanism explains.

**Ondel et al. (ICASSP 2017), "Bayesian phonotactic language model for AUD"** (not reached in full text; secondary sources report +1-2 NMI absolute). Note this is context in the **prior** (bigram over units), not in the emission. Our prior is already trigram, i.e. strictly stronger than the thing that produced that gain.

**Ondel, Burget et al. (Interspeech 2019), "Bayesian Subspace HMM for AUD", arXiv:1904.03876.** Directly states our failure mode from the likelihood side: "the AUD algorithm may maximize the likelihood while modeling non-phonetic information" (Sec 2.1), and "most of the AUD algorithms are prone to model speaker/channel or any non-phonetic variability" (Sec 1). Their remedy is the **opposite** of ours: *constrain* the generative model, forcing each unit's HMM parameters into a 30-100 dim "phonetic subspace" learned from other languages ("we force the model to learn 'what is a phone'"). 49.2% eq. PER on TIMIT with MBN features; the 3861-dim parameter space compresses to 30 dims "and yet achieving the same PER as the HMM baseline". Cost: cross-lingual phone supervision (German/Polish/Spanish labels). Verdict: expressiveness of the reverse model is not the bottleneck; *inductive bias about what a phone is* is.

**wav2vec-U (Baevski et al., JMLR/NeurIPS 2021), arXiv:2105.11084.** Architecture facts, verified: generator = "a single non-causal convolution with kernel size 4" (~90k params); discriminator = 3 causal conv blocks, kernel 6, **receptive field 16 segments**. So the field's working system has (a) context on the *recognizer* side, (b) a prior with ~16-symbol context, far beyond a trigram, and (c) no generative reverse model at all. Our trigram prior is weak by this standard.

**Klejch, Wallington, Bell (ICASSP 2022), "Deciphering Speech", arXiv:2111.06799.** Noisy-channel decipherment: Plex(X|Y,A) is a per-symbol, context-INDEPENDENT substitution table (further *pruned* to top-10 phones per grapheme). Verbatim: "The Language model is the component most important to decipherment, providing information to the training process." Their schedule raises the prior's order progressively (bigram -> 5-gram character, then a **word** trigram LM with a grapheme lexicon for the final round) because "using n-gram models with large contexts results in a big composition ... therefore it is not feasible to use them from the beginning". Reads on 20 min of speech. Verdict: in the closest structural analogue to our setup (lattice, WFST, emission table, symbolic prior), *all* the identifiability comes from the prior side; the emission stays context-free on purpose.

**Lee, O'Donnell, Glass (TACL 2015), "Unsupervised Lexicon Discovery from Acoustic Input", https://aclanthology.org/Q15-1028.pdf.** Ablation -NC ("no noisy-channel", i.e. remove the phone-level substitution/insertion/deletion model) degrades word-discovery F1 consistently (e.g. Economics FullDP 16.1 -> -AM 14.9 -> -NC 13.8; Signal processing 18.3 -> 17.0 -> 14.5). Their words: the ability "to successfully unify multiple acoustic sequences into single lexical items relies on the phonological-variability (noisy channel) component ... demonstrating the importance of modeling symbolic variation in phonological units." Again a *context-independent* symbolic variability model, plus a **word-level (lexicon) prior**, is what works.

## Q2. When the prior/discriminator is satisfied by a non-phone code, what fixed it?

**wav2vec-U 2.0 (Liu, Hsu, Baevski, Mohamed, 2022), arXiv:2204.02492.** States our failure mode almost verbatim (Sec 3.3): "The generator can possibly learn to satisfy the adversarial criterion without learning the correct mapping - for example, by consistently producing the most common n-grams regardless of the input speech." Fix: an **auxiliary self-supervised loss** L_ss = -sum_t log P_G(z_t | X) predicting MFCC k-means pseudo-labels from an *intermediate generator layer*. Effect (Table 1/2, LibriSpeech dev-other, greedy, 8 runs): 15.9 +- 1.1 -> **13.6 +- 0.9** avg PER with 64 MFCC k-means classes. Stated rationale: "content-based regularization to ensure the output of the generator is closely related to the input speech".
**Critical caveat for us:** their degeneracy is *input-independent* ("regardless of the input speech"). Ours is *input-dependent but content-free*. Our code would already satisfy L_ss. This fix does not transfer.
**Second, under-cited result in the same table:** richer pseudo-labels are WORSE. wav2vec2.0 VQ indices (320x2) give 16.6 +- 2.2, i.e. *below the 15.9 no-aux baseline*; MFCC k-means 128 classes gives 16.8 vs 64 classes 13.6. Their explanation: "a larger label set will encode redundant information in the raw audio". Our K=500 discrete acoustic codes sit on the wrong side of this curve.
Also: output frequency matters more than architecture — "models with higher output frequency ... failed to converge ... having output frequency close to ground truth (around 10 Hz) is important."

**wav2vec-U 1.0's own answers to degeneracy, none of them emission context:** (i) a phoneme **diversity** penalty + a **segment smoothness** penalty; (ii) an unsupervised cross-validation metric that explicitly checks **vocabulary usage** — "Measuring vocabulary usage identifies degenerate models which output fluent but trivial transcriptions" — and they note "trivial solutions may achieve very low entropy", counteracted by flooring LM entropy at the training-data entropy; (iii) brute-force **selection**: 5 seeds per config, 40 models, and reported mean +- std over **20 random seeds**; (iv) **aligning the text side's marginals to the audio side** — inserting SIL at the start/end of every phonemized sentence and randomly between words at rate 0.25 (Fig 5, ~24 -> ~20 PER on dev-other); (v) **self-training** (3-stage HMM + fine-tune) for the large remaining gap.

**REBORN (Tseng et al. 2024), arXiv:2402.03988.** Trains a segmenter by RL against a perplexity-difference reward. Verbatim failure: "using only the perplexity difference reward will make the segmentation model predict segments overly focusing on lowering PPL. And a phoneme sequence with a lower perplexity may not always be a better transcription"; Appendix A.2: "using perplexity (PPL) alone as a reward yields the lowest perplexity scores upon model convergence. However ... the PER increases when the PPL reward is the sole metric ... the model tends to minimize perplexity by producing more lengthy and less accurate transcriptions." This is exactly "the text prior is satisfiable by a non-phone code", measured. Their fix: two **regularization rewards** — edit distance to the *previous iteration's* transcription (c_edit=0.2) and a length-difference reward — i.e. anchor to an already-grounded hypothesis; plus behaviour-cloning init. Payoff is from **segmentation**, not emission context: first segmenter iteration drops PER by 6.4 abs vs wav2vec-U; over iterations the phoneme predictor evaluated on *oracle* segments goes 13.8% -> 7.4% PER (>45% rel). Notable: "the segmental structures are acoustic units **smaller than phonemes**, which helps the phoneme prediction model" — segments need not equal phones.

**Chen et al. (Interspeech 2019), arXiv:1904.04100** (partially read): GAN + iteratively refined HMMs (Algorithm 1: train GAN -> decode T -> train HMMs -> re-derive boundaries b by forced alignment), plus an **intra-segment loss** homogenizing frame posteriors within a segment. 33.1% PER TIMIT. HMMs are triphone+LDA+MLLT, but again as a self-training back-end downstream of the GAN, not the identifying objective. *Full-text verification of the mono/tri contrast here: NOT REACHED.*

## Q3. Risk side — a more expressive generative model making the code LESS phone-like

**Chorowski, Weiss, Bengio, van den Oord (IEEE/ACM TASLP 2019), "Unsupervised speech representation learning using WaveNet autoencoders", arXiv:1901.08810.** The decisive paper on this side.
- Mechanism, stated (Sec II-C): "Training mixed latent variable and autoregressive models is prone to latent space collapse, in which the decoder learns to ignore the constrained latent representations and only uses the unconstrained signal coming through the autoregressive path." Design principle (Sec III): "this bottleneck encourages the encoder to discard portions of the latent representation which the decoder can infer from the two other information streams."
- **Measured non-monotonicity in decoder context (Fig 6):** "Impact of decoder WaveNet receptive field on the properties of the VQ-VAE conditioning signal. The representation is significantly more gender invariant when the receptive field is larger than 10ms. Frame-wise phoneme recognition accuracy **peaks at about 125ms**." i.e. phone-likeness of the code rises then falls as the generative model's context grows. There is an optimum, and it is not "more".
- **The fix they needed was a capacity *limit* on the generative path, not more of it:** time-jitter regularization (randomly replace a latent by a neighbour, p=0.12). Table III, LibriSpeech, 256 tokens, 25 Hz, 200k steps: none 52.5 -> time-jitter p=0.12 **56.2** frame phoneme accuracy; at 50 Hz, none 46.5 -> 56.1. Regular dropout p=0.1/0.2 *hurt* (50.7/49.1). Rationale: "limiting the amount of information on the autoregressive path".
- **The nuisance-conditioning principle, which is the one that bites us:** they condition the decoder on speaker and show the code becomes speaker-invariant, citing Moyer et al.'s proof that "this approach produces representations that are invariant to the explicitly provided information". Generalised: *whatever you hand the reverse model, the code stops having to encode.*

## What this changes for our decision

1. **The proposed direction is not the one the field used for our symptom, and the closest direct test is null.** Yeh et al. is the only mono-vs-tri contrast in an unsupervised phone recognizer, and under a non-matching text corpus it is 44.7 -> 44.9. Our setup is non-matching by construction.
2. **The mechanism argues the other way.** p_phi(units | k, h) hands h to the reverse model. By the Chorowski/Moyer principle, information recoverable from h is information the code no longer has to place in k. Since h is itself a code symbol, a context-dependent reverse model is *more* satisfiable by a non-phone partition, not less: an arbitrary partition with strong sequential structure (exactly what a confident, input-dependent, content-free code is) gains predictive power from h. The claim "a segment-independent reverse model is satisfiable by a non-phone code while a coarticulation-aware one favours the phone partition" has no published support and one published mechanism against it.
3. **What the field actually did about our exact symptom, in order of evidence strength:** (a) strengthen and *lexicalise* the prior (Klejch: char bigram -> 5-gram -> word trigram + lexicon; Lee 2015: lexicon + noisy channel; wav2vec-U: 16-segment discriminator RF). Our trigram is the weakest prior in this whole literature. (b) fix the boundaries / segmentation and put a duration-ish or smoothness constraint on the code (REBORN: -6.4 PER in one iteration; Yeh: 49.1 -> 42.6; Yeh frame-smoothness ablation; wav2vec-U L_sp; Chen intra-segment loss). (c) constrain, do not expand, the generative model (SHMM 30-dim phonetic subspace). (d) match the text side's marginals to the audio side (SIL insertion, ~4 PER). (e) select across seeds with a degeneracy detector (vocabulary usage), then self-train.
4. **Two operating-point warnings that survive transfer.** Our K=500 code inventory is on the wrong side of wav2vec-U 2.0's pseudo-label curve (64 best, 128 worse, 320x2 VQ worse than no aux at all: "a larger label set will encode redundant information in the raw audio"). And output rate near ~10 Hz mattered more than architecture there.
5. **If we still want to run it, the pre-registration the literature implies.** Do not gate on likelihood or on the prior's satisfaction — both rose in every failure above. Gate on a phone-likeness read that is independent of the objective (frame NMI with phones, or PER), and run the context-dependent and context-independent reverse models as a *pair* at matched prior order and matched segmentation, because Chorowski Fig 6 says the effect of generative context is non-monotone and therefore a single point cannot settle it. Budget a third arm that instead raises the prior (4-gram or a lexicon/word-level term) — that is where the reproduced gains are.
