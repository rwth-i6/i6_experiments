# Design review, P1 corrupted-phi lift ladder (2026-09-25, before any job)

Verdict: **DONE_WITH_CONCERNS.** The design measures the claim (rt_r70 reproduces JUPITER's node R; rt_r80 / rt_r90 are single-delta extensions in rho), and the ported code does what the Design says. Three things would waste the cohort or leave the boundary claim resting on the wrong bed, and each has a cheap fix in the gate text or the launch. MUST-fix items are 1-4; the rest are optional or report-only.

Read: `SAE_i6_P1.md`, `SAE_i6.md`, `SAE_i6_ref.md` s2/s6, `SAE_i6_ref_lexlat_v2.md` (L2-0, A13, ladder read), `SAE_i6_P0.md` (Deviations, Gates, Results), `exp_logs/SAE/SAE_4A_lexlat_v2.md` (L2-0, A4, A5, A7, A13, Results), `reverse_model/ladder.py`, `supervised.py`, `supervised_data.py`, `config/lexlat_v2.py`, `training/{config,jobs,arms,schedules}.py`, `model/{lexlat_k2,lexlat_k2_train,blankfree_model}.py`, `tests/test_reverse_ladder.py`, `tests/test_model_lexlat_k2.py`, `settings.py`, the i6 HLG `build.json`, the k2lat job log, the pinned RETURNN engine. One bounded numeric check (numpy nesting) was run in the scratchpad; nothing on disk was changed except this file.

## Findings, most material first

### 1. MUST: memory and GPU. The default route sends the arms to a 32 GB V100, and JUPITER's own numbers say the recipe needs 80 GB unless the k2 backward is made per chunk

- `settings.py:396-400`: every `ReturnnTrainingJob` run task, resumes included, goes to `gpu_32gb` (V100 32 GB) unless the rqmt already carries an explicit `-p` (`settings.py:394`). `training/jobs.py:27,87` sets `gpu_mem = 96`, which is not passed to Slurm. So as specified, rt_r70/80/90 launch on a 32 GB card.
- JUPITER (`SAE_4A_lexlat_v2.md` A13, lines 128-136): peak reserved 80.3 GB (rt_r70), 81.7 (rt_r100), 83.7 (cold_ctl) of 95 GB, set at steps 1-2 while the lattices are near-uniform (37,200 arcs/frame), and "chunking does not bound held memory". The bed step alone allocates 20.28 GiB (reserved 28.2 on V100, 36.8 on L40S; `SAE_i6_P0.md:313-315`), so the k2 leg holds roughly 45-55 GB at the flat start. The largest i6 GPU is the L40S with 46 GB. The training path keeps every chunk's autograd graph (`model/lexlat_k2_train.py:480-490` calls `chunked_tot_scores` with `backward=False`), so `lexlat_k2_chunk_seqs` cannot lower the peak. Prediction: an OOM at steps 0-3 on any i6 GPU as the code stands; on a V100 with certainty.
- The fix A13 planned and never applied is implementation-only: per-chunk backward with gradient accumulation. The chunk loop already has that path (`model/lexlat_k2.py:1041-1052`, `backward=True` frees each chunk's graph; used by the probe). In the train step it needs a detached `dense_in` leaf, the per-sequence scale (`-w_i / retained_i / n_keep`, lam) applied to each chunk's `z_hlg` slice before its backward, the monitors read per chunk before the lattice is dropped, and the surrogate `(log_q * dense_in.grad).sum()` added to the loss so RETURNN's backward carries the gradient into theta. Gradient identical to the held path (chunks write disjoint slices of one grad tensor), verifiable by a T1.21-style test (`tests/test_model_lexlat_k2.py:399-419` already shows chunk 1/2/16 agree to 1e-9 in log Z and 1e-7 in gradient). No registered constant changes; it is not a new operating point.
- Amendment (exact text for G1.M): "The rt arms run on `gpu_48gb` (L40S 46 GB) with an explicit `-p gpu_48gb` in the job rqmt (hash-neutral; resumes keep it, `settings.py:394`). Before any arm, a probe runs the FULL rt config of the most exposed arm to be launched (rt_r90, or rt_r100 if item 3 adds it; weaker phis keep the lattice wide longer, A13's ordering) on one L40S for sub-epoch 1, with `torch_log_memory_usage = True` in the config's `post_config` (hash-neutral; per-step peak allocated, `recipe/returnn/returnn/torch/engine.py:135,1603`), the k2 monitor `lexlat_k2_peak_reserved_gib`, and `nvidia-smi` on the node. PASS = no OOM, no k2 int32 error, no `lexlat_k2_ABORT.json`, the stability read at sub-epoch 1 completes, and peak allocated <= 40 GiB over ALL of sub-epoch 1 (not steps 0-3: A13 set its peaks at steps 1-2 on short batches and the longest batches came at steps 7-8 with the lattice still wide for weak phis). If the probe OOMs or exceeds 40 GiB, the per-chunk backward is implemented, unit-tested against the held path (log Z 1e-9, gradient 1e-7) and code-reviewed, then the probe reruns. A passed probe may continue as that arm's run." Given the JUPITER numbers, implementing the per-chunk backward BEFORE the probe is the cheaper order; the probe then confirms rather than discovers.
- Time on L40S: the bed step is 68 s (`SAE_i6_P0.md:313`), plus the k2 leg; expect 80-100 s/step, 57 steps, so 1.3-1.6 h per sub-epoch and 11-14 h per arm. Inside the 72 h cap; three or four arms fit the 5-GPU L40S cap once P0's ctrl_20 ends (about 19:00).

### 2. MUST: G1.L has no rule for a non-monotone ladder and no VOID rule for an aborted arm

- `SAE_i6_P1.md:49-52`: "r80 not LIFT: boundary in (0.7, 0.8]" reads that branch even if r90 LIFTs. JUPITER's A4 rule (`SAE_4A_lexlat_v2.md` A4) says non-monotone = CANNOT_TELL. Amendment: "If r80 does not LIFT and r90 does, the read is CANNOT_TELL and both rungs get a second seed (flat seed 1 / random_seed 1 / offset 1000, `ladder.SECOND_SEED`) before any boundary is claimed."
- `model/lexlat_k2_train.py:562-567,658-700`: more than 10 % empty lattices in one batch writes `lexlat_k2_ABORT.json` and exits 0 (JUPITER saw 2 empty lattices in 100 steps at chunk 4; the i6 graph is 5 % larger). Such an arm has no ep8 checkpoint and would otherwise be read as missing, or worse as NO LIFT. Amendment: "An arm with an abort marker or without its ep8 checkpoint reads VOID, never NO LIFT, and is rerun after diagnosis."

### 3. MUST (gate text): the "both LIFT" branch rests on JUPITER's r100, i.e. on a different prior, graph and unit inventory

- `SAE_i6_P1.md:52`: "Both LIFT: boundary in (0.9, 1.0], resting on JUPITER's r100." On i6 there would then be no arm that reads NO LIFT, so nothing shows the i6 bed (full-text prior ppl 9.60, 182,215-pronunciation HLG vs 151,731, i6 units) cannot lift a content-free fitted phi. JUPITER's r100 read is robust (0.8552, with cold_ctl 0.8487 and rt_perm 0.8655), so this is a cross-bed extrapolation rather than a likely error; but a design that claims an i6 boundary should not need it. The branches "r80 not LIFT" and "r90 not LIFT" carry their own non-lifting rung and need no control.
- Amendment: "If r80 and r90 both LIFT, rt_r100 (rho 1.0, the same recipe; the nested corruption's last rung) runs on i6 before the claim. r100 NO LIFT: boundary in (0.9, 1.0] on i6. r100 PARTIAL or LIFT: VOID; either the i6 bed lifts a content-free phi or the corruption is defective, and the debugger reads the r100 fit's dev NLL and its corruption.json first." Conditional, so it costs one run only in the branch that needs it. A cold_ctl is not needed for P1's question.

### 4. MUST: the P1 entry point must build exactly the P1 graph, and the per-arm delta must be checked as a config diff

- `config/lexlat_v2.py:76-109` (`ladder()`) builds `RHOS = (0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 1.0)` fits plus permphi, the competence reads and all eight `RT_PACKS` arms (`ladder.py:380,397-402`). Used as is, it launches five fits, eight 8-sub-epoch arms and the reads: a wasted cohort. `RT_PACKS` is hard-coded, so rt_r80 / rt_r90 need a new builder (a `build_rt(arms=...)` or a P1-local dict). The code review before launch should confirm, from the written `returnn.config` files, that rt_r80 and rt_r90 differ from rt_r70 in `reverse_checkpoint_path` only, and that rt_r70 carries `lexlat_k2_chunk_seqs 4`, `onset 1`, `max_active 1000`, `ramp 3`, tau `[2.0]*8`, lr `[1e-5] + [1e-4]*7`, the flat init seed 0 and no `sil_run_collapse` (the `tests/test_reverse_ladder.py:110-138` assertions, applied to the real configs).
- The G0.G implementer is editing `ladder.py` (reads only, `reports/impl_g0g_core_phi_reads_2026-09-25.md`); the rt section (`ladder.py:611-698`) is unchanged by it. One writer per file: the P1 implementer waits, as State says.

### 5. Fidelity of rt_r70 (question 1): reproduced by construction, with these differences

Same as JUPITER's node R (`ladder.py:611-678`, `tests/test_reverse_ladder.py:110-138`): tau held at 2.0 for 8 sub-epochs; lr 1e-5 then 1e-4 (the N = 20 list truncated); k2 block rung 1000 / on-set 1 / ramp 3 / lam 1 / beams 20-8-30 on the in-house word-boundary graph (`get_graph("inhouse_3gram")`, the same `LexlatHLGBuildJob.avjHv1Xvjyqd` k2lat trains on, per its log line 353); chunk 4; flat init `FlatRecognizerInitJob(seed=0)` (zero output logits, `training/init.py:16-20`); phi = the fit's epoch 8; both trainable; kept 1/2/4/8; 88,000 / 128 batches; float64 DP; the banked SIL-split lattice (`training/config.py:213` defaults `sil_run_collapse` False and `rt_train_config` does not set it). The per-sub-epoch k2 stability read at rung 10000 is chunked at 1 utterance (`stability_chunk_seqs 1` in the k2lat log), so JUPITER's int32 pre-flight fix is in the port. The corruption is the ported job: exactly round(rho n_u) positions from one permutation, symbols from the renormalised seed unigram, rng from (seed, tag) only (`ladder.py:124-171`); nesting across rho holds (`tests/test_reverse_ladder.py:34`; also checked numerically here for rho 0.7/0.8/0.9/1.0 on several n: nested).

Differences, all inherited from P0 and named in `SAE_i6_P1.md:33-34`: i6 prior (ppl 9.60 vs 9.56), i6 HLG (182,215 pronunciations, 24.9 M states, 103.2 M arcs vs 151,731 / 23.9 M / 98.6 M), i6 audio label (features, k-means codebook and eta all refit), the gold-phi recipe as a RETURNN job (G0.R3 PASS at 3.274 vs 3.289), hardware, one job per arm instead of a 4-GPU pack. Two are NOT in the P1 file and should be added to Design:
- the lattice keeps the banked SIL split, not P0's rc base (P0 says rc is the base of every new arm from P0 on; P1 is a reproduction and must say it keeps the banked behaviour);
- CANNOT_TELL from i6: `SAE_4A_lexlat_v2.md` L2-0 Inputs says "length kept; run-collapse may shorten a string", while the ported `CorruptSeedGoldJob` never collapses (`ladder.py:14-15,38`, collapse count 0 by construction) and the fit loader accepts adjacent repeats (`supervised_data.py:72-83` checks feasibility only). If JUPITER's job collapsed runs created by substitution, its r70 strings were slightly shorter and its phi slightly different. The JUPITER orchestrator can settle it from the source (`blankfree_ladder_jobs.py`, c49559ce); on i6, `corruption.json` reports `adjacent_repeats_corrupted` minus `adjacent_repeats_gold`, which bounds the effect (record it). Small; not a blocker.

rt_r80 and rt_r90 differ from rt_r70 in rho alone (same seed, nested draws, same fit recipe, same arm config), subject to item 4's config diff.

### 6. G1.R70 (question 2): the window cannot discriminate, so make it Tier B

- 0.1909 +-0.02 spans 0.171-0.211, which contains JUPITER's rt_r0 (0.1776), r30 (0.1826), r50 (0.1863) and r70 (0.1909). A pass therefore says "LIFT to about gold level", not "the r70 rung reproduces". Its informative outcome is a miss (a slower lift), and r70 is the rung most sensitive to the bed (its ep1 is 0.2533 against 0.18-0.19 for the others). Five disclosed deviations act on it, and there is no i6 identity band in the lifted regime (the banked warm bands are 0.001-0.002: `supphi_k2lat_rep`, rt_r0_s2).
- Amendment: "G1.R70 (A): rt_r70 reads LIFT at ep8 and ep8 < ep1. (B): ep8 0.1909 +-0.02, ep1 0.2533 +-0.03; a B miss goes to the debugger before the verdict and may be attributed to the disclosed deviations (prior, graph, audio, refit, hardware) with evidence; on an attributed miss G1.L is still read, and the phase records that the i6 boundary is not comparable to JUPITER's at the 0.02 level." Leaving the window Tier A would void r80 / r90 on an attributable bed effect.
- The A4 bands transfer: i6 ctrl_20 reads 0.855-0.885 at ep1-10 (`SAE_i6_P0.md:299,344`), inside the band the PARTIAL threshold was derived from, and LIFT < 0.50 sits 0.3 from both regimes. r30 and r50 need no i6 rerun: every branch of G1.L is decided by r70/r80/r90 (and r100 under item 3).
- Seeds: one per rung is enough while every rung lands where JUPITER's did (LIFT <= 0.19 or NO LIFT >= 0.85). Optional rule for the decisive rung: "If the largest lifting rung's successor reads PARTIAL or NO LIFT, that successor gets a second seed before the boundary is final; if the seeds disagree, the read is CANNOT_TELL at that rung." One conditional run.

### 7. G1.F: add a check that the fits are not the gold phi, and name the nesting reader

Cheap gate clause: "Each fit's `dev_loss_nll_per_frame` at epoch 8 (its held 28 utterances carry the same corruption) is above the i6 gold phi's 3.2742 (G0.R3) and rises with rho: r70 < r80 < r90 (< r100 if run). A violation is a Tier-A FAIL of the fit." Report-only beside it: the competence statistic S on the 260 set for the three phis (the `competence_reads` builder, after G0.G lands; JUPITER r70 4.411, r100 4.690), expected monotone in rho.

An `analysis/` script (implementer) compares the three `corrupted_phones.json` against the gold json: for (r70, r80) and (r80, r90), the differing positions of the smaller rho are a subset of the larger's and carry the same symbol; realised rate = parts.all.realised_substitution_rate. Pure Python, seconds.

### 8. Question 3 details: chunk size, allocator, GPU

- `lexlat_k2_chunk_seqs` below 4 is numerics-neutral in this code: k2 prunes, beams and caps active states per sequence (`model/lexlat_k2.py:891-899`), T1.21 pins chunk 1/2/16 to 1e-9 / 1e-7, and the empty-lattice rule is per batch, not per chunk (`lexlat_k2_train.py:566`). It does not lower the peak (item 1). `PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF=expandable_segments:True` reduces fragmentation only and moves no number; fine for the probe and the arms, disclose it.
- Int32 overflow on the i6 graph: the worst-case bound is always above 2^31 (word-boundary fan-out of the undeterminized escape graph), so `chunk_guard` only warns; the real count at rung 1000 / chunk 4 scaled to about 6e7 on JUPITER's graph, and the i6 graph has 20 % more pronunciations. The probe's first steps and its stability read (rung 10000, chunk 1, 16 utterances) are the test; the training log shows a k2 `Array1::Init Check failed: size >= 0` if it fails.
- GPU choice: L40S (item 1). A V100 becomes possible only if, with the per-chunk backward, the probe's peak allocated is well under 24 GiB (the bed alone reserves 28 GiB on V100), which is unlikely; do not plan on it.
